U.S. National Trust Bank Charter Applications — State of Play

A survey of pending, approved, and denied national trust bank (NTB) charter applications before the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), compiled from public information. Fintechs and digital-asset firms have surged toward the NTB charter — a federal pathway for custody and (under the GENIUS Act) stablecoin activity that eases state-by-state oversight, though trust banks cannot lend or take deposits. Proposed-activities detail and conditions are drawn from each entity's OCC decision letter or application filing; every point is hyperlinked to its source.

Why some receipt dates are "APPROX." An OCC corporate decision letter states the decision date and the OCC control number, but usually not the application receipt date. A firm receipt date comes from one of three places: (1) the "Date Received" column of the OCC's Digital Assets Licensing Applications index; (2) the date stamped on the application document in the public volume; or (3) a decision letter that happens to recite "On [date], [applicant] filed…" (as BitGo's and Paxos's letters do). For four applicants — Fidelity, Crypto.com, Bridge, and Laser Digital — none of those yields a date, so the value is inferred from the OCC control-number filing year plus the press announcement date (which is the announcement, not the OCC's receipt). To make any of these exact, pull that applicant's OCC Corporate Applications Search (CAS) record by filing ID — the same source that pins Wise to June 16, 2025 — or the OCC weekly bulletin's "applications received" listing for the relevant week. Those are query interfaces, not static pages, so they weren't captured in the research pass.

The "five" (OCC News Release 2025-125, Dec 12, 2025). Conditionally approved in one batch: Circle, Ripple (de novo) and BitGo, Fidelity, Paxos (conversions). A structural contrast: Circle and Ripple do not issue a stablecoin at the bank — the bank only manages the reserve "on a directed basis" and acts as collateral trustee, while the affiliated NY trust company issues USDC/RLUSD. BitGo, Fidelity, and Paxos each propose to issue a stablecoin at the bank. Circle has reached final approval (July 10, 2026); the other four remain conditional/pre-opening. Note the capital conditions scale with the business: from ~$6M (Circle, Laser Digital) up to $60M (Coinbase).

Cross-cutting roadblocks worth watching. Two structural headwinds cut across the pipeline: (1) the Federal Reserve's May 2026 pause on master-account access for uninsured national trust banks — cited by Wise as making its original model non-viable; and (2) an organized opposition campaign by ICBA (urging the OCC to pause all NTB approvals), the Bank Policy Institute, and NCRC, all challenging the OCC's statutory authority to charter uninsured, non-fiduciary crypto trust banks outside the Bank Holding Company Act framework.

Method & sourcing. Proposed-activities points and conditions are quoted or paraphrased from each entity's OCC materials: for the approved/conditional cohort, the OCC decision letters (News Release 2025-125 letters a–e; Corporate Decisions #1365 Bridge, #1367 Foris DAX/Crypto.com, #1370 Coinbase, #1372 Mercury, #1377 Laser Digital, #1378 Morgan Stanley); for pending applicants, the OCC application public volumes / public-comment filings (and, where the business plan is confidential, the BPI comment letter that quotes it — noted inline for Agora and Bastion). Dates and status are additionally sourced to the OCC Digital Assets Licensing Applications index and the OCC Corporate Applications Search (CAS/CATS), plus reputable press and company filings. Each cell links its sources.

Why four receipt dates are APPROX. OCC decision letters state the decision date and control number but usually not the receipt date; a firm date needs the OCC "Date Received" index, the application document's date, or a letter reciting "On [date]…filed." For Fidelity (letter omits the filing-date line; no public volume; the biggest gap), Crypto.com/Foris DAX (~Oct 2025, from control # 2025-Charter-343659 + comment timing), Stripe/Bridge (~Oct 15, 2025, from a co-founder announcement + docket OCC-2025-0603), and Laser Digital (~late Jan 2026, from control # 2026-Charter-344710 + announcement), no such source is public. Each can be pinned exactly via that applicant's OCC Corporate Applications Search (CAS) record by filing ID, or the OCC weekly bulletin "applications received" listing — both query interfaces rather than static pages.

Conditions note. All approved/conditional entries are OCC preliminary conditional approvals; the conditions listed are the material ones from each decision letter (capital minimums, liquid-asset floors, 180-day expense reserves, BSA/AML programs, senior-officer vetting, GENIUS-Act conformance, and — for several — a 12-month capital / 18-month opening expiry). The letters contain additional standard pre-opening requirements not all itemized here; the linked letter is authoritative. Pending applicants have no conditions yet — those attach only at approval.
